PRIDE Peace Restoration and Integral Development Initiative, Borno State, Nigeria
Frontline

The beginning · Early May 2026

A local organization in Nigeria sees a cholera outbreak happening

PRIDE works through volunteers and health workers from the communities they serve. It hears about the cholera threat from community leaders, health facilities and its own workers.

It went differently last time

When flooding hit northern Nigeria in September 2024, affecting more than 150,000 households, PRIDE identified the need and activated its network. External funding arrived months later, after the emergency had worsened.

This time, they wanted it to go differently.

Connect Connect Connect

25 May 2026

The local organization takes action

PRIDE submits a concept note through Connect: their idea, a budget, and a plan to put it into action quickly.

Funders

16 June 2026

Matched, and funded

Within days the plan and service package were agreed, and matched with a donor family, Annie and Jacob Ma-Weaver.

$20,000USD to support 4,500 household visits

17 June 2026

9 days to the first visit

A standing agreement with the Borno State Ministry of Health meant PRIDE only had to notify the authorities, not apply for fresh approvals. It spent the days before launch briefing households.

Frontline

26 June – 31 July 2026

Door-to-door campaigns start

Frontline workers travel door-to-door across four government areas of Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camps. Each visit included five interventions:

  • Hygiene promotion
  • Household water treatment
  • Oral rehydration solution (ORS) and zinc for early treatment of diarrhoea
  • Counselling on routine childhood immunization
  • Screening for cholera symptoms, with health facility referrals
Frontline
“

One night, at around 2:00 a.m., a neighbor knocked at my door because her child was showing symptoms of cholera. Because I had received ORS through the PRIDE-supported response, I was able to provide it immediately while encouraging the family to seek appropriate medical care. The ORS helped relieve the symptoms, and the family later told me that the support provided at that odd hour had helped save their child's life.

A mother in Fariya camp, JereAs shared by Moses Tizhe, PRIDE's Executive Director

From 26 June 2026

Connect tracks on‑the‑ground activities in real time

Every visit is recorded and verified in real time. PRIDE is paid only for visits that are verified. Throughout, PRIDE coaches frontline workers as the campaign runs, and funders can see what is being delivered and paid for.

Frontline
“

The platform supported our field teams to collect and manage data more efficiently, monitor activities, and strengthen accountability during a fast-moving emergency.

Moses TizheExecutive Director, PRIDE

Meanwhile · 23 July 2026

By late July, Médecins Sans Frontières (Doctors Without Borders) reported that suspected cases had surged past 35,500, with 198 deaths. PRIDE was already funded and at work.

The end result

  1. $20,000 80% to the frontline organization 20% Connect platform fee
  2. 5,187 Households reached 10,338 oral rehydration solution (ORS) co-packs and 5,187 water-treatment sachets delivered
